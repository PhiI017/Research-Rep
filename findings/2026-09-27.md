## 3. Theory test — the staged margin plan

**The theory:** borrow on margin in 25% pieces when the S&P closes 10/15/20/25% below its
all-time high, because buying dips beats the borrowing cost.

**Test:** daily S&P 500 closes 1960-01-04 to 2026-09-11 from this repo's store
(`data/spx_daily.csv`). Price only — dividends add roughly 2%/yr to
every number below, trigger or not [G]. Each level fires once per drawdown episode (from a high
until a new high). Script: `scripts/margin_test.py`.

**Forward S&P return per year after each trigger, compared with buying on any day:**
- Any day: 1y median 10.5%, 3y 8.6%, 5y 8.6%; beat 8% over 5y in 53% of cases
- **-10%** (22 episodes): 1y 11.4%, 3y 9.4%, **5y 7.0%**; beat 8% over 5y in 8 of 20
- **-15%** (14): 1y 9.2%, 3y 6.3%, **5y 5.8%**; beat 8% over 5y in 5 of 12
- **-20%** (10): 1y 19.8%, 3y 10.6%, 5y 6.1%; beat 8% over 5y in 4 of 9
- **-25%** (9): 1y 22.9%, 3y 10.5%, **5y 9.8%**; beat 8% over 5y in 5 of 8

**What happened after the trigger** — how much further the S&P fell:
- -10%: median another 10%, worst another 52% (Nov 2007); 6 of 22 fell another 20%+
- -15%: median 14%, worst 49%; 5 of 14 fell another 20%+
- -20%: median 9%, worst 46%; 3 of 10
- -25%: median 10%, worst 42% (Sept 2008); 3 of 9

**Result [C]:**
1. **The -10% and -15% triggers did not beat buying on an ordinary day** over 3 and 5 years.
   A -10% fall is too common to carry information: 22 times in 66 years.
2. **The -20% and -25% triggers did better over 1-3 years**, and -25% was the only level whose
   worst 5-year result was positive (+1.4%/yr). The samples are small (9-10 episodes), so this
   is suggestive, not proven.
3. **At an 8% margin rate the plan loses to the borrowing cost about half the time at every
   level.** At 5% it wins in roughly 6 of 10 cases, before dividends.
4. **The real risk is the further fall, not the average.** In about 1 case in 4, a borrow at
   -10% was followed by another 20%+ drop. Your collateral is SCHG, whose worst drawdown was
   -34.6% against the S&P's -25% in 2022 [V from summary], so your collateral falls faster than
   the index that triggers the borrowing.

**What this suggests [O]:** if you keep the plan, weight it toward the deep levels (for
example, nothing at -10%, more at -20/-25%), and size it by asking: "if the S&P falls another
50% after my last borrow, do I get a margin call?" Your borrowing rate matters as much as the
trigger.
