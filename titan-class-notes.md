## Unit 1: The core idea

Two systems, two jobs.

- The core is SCHG plus value picks like Adobe, judged by your four-step method.
- The titan sleeve is a small, fixed slice of the portfolio judged by the blueprint.

Your value method will reject almost every early titan (Amazon in 2002 failed most pillars).
That's expected, not a flaw.

Starting size decides the ceiling. 100x from a $45 billion company means a $4.5 trillion
company, which is nearly impossible. AMD started around $2 billion and NVIDIA around $10–15
billion. Hunt in the $500 million to $5 billion range.

By the time everyone calls it "the next NVIDIA," most of the move is gone. AST SpaceMobile
(~30x) and Rocket Lab (~17x) had already run before we looked.

Think second-order. Everyone watched AI chips, but the grid equipment behind them was the
bottleneck. The early money goes to whoever supplies what the obvious story can't live without.

## Unit 2: The nine-point blueprint

1. A wave bigger than the company
2. A real product already selling, with growth accelerating
3. A moat that strengthens with size
4. Rising gross margins (pricing power)
5. A long-horizon leader who owns meaningful stock
6. Reinvestment over short-term profit, with healthy unit economics
7. Customers who stay and spend more
8. Survivable dilution and a cash runway
9. Small starting size

## Unit 3: Six signals that an industry is about to boom

1. Big buyers commit spending before supply exists
2. Lead times stretch
3. Unit costs fall fast
4. Adoption sits around 1–10%
5. The first pure-play turns profitable
6. The sector's stocks haven't spiked yet

## Unit 4: Reading the numbers (vocabulary to master)

- **Operating leverage:** fixed costs make profits swing harder than sales in both directions.
  PLPC's sales rose 25% and EPS rose 75%; in 2024, sales fell 11% and profit fell 41%.
- **Gross margin trend:** rising means pricing power; falling during growth means the company
  is buying growth or ramping new capacity.
- **Backlog and book-to-bill:** a falling backlog can be healthy if it's being shipped.
  Book-to-bill above 1.0 means demand is still outrunning output.
- **Adjusted vs. reported earnings:** trust adjusted numbers only if the gap closes over time.
- **Which P/E:** trailing, forward, and run-rate can differ by 2x. The debate is always whether
  the current pace is the new normal or the peak.
- **Organic vs. acquired growth:** strip out acquisitions and currency effects to see the real
  engine.
- **Cyclical vs. structural:** check 10+ years of revenue. Four percent a year with busts means
  cyclical, no matter how good this year looks.
- **Share count:** check whether a market cap includes all share classes. Rising share counts
  mean dilution.
- **Ownership:** look past the CEO to whoever actually controls the votes, and check
  dual-class structures.

## Unit 5: Holding rules

- Expect 50–90% drawdowns. Amazon, Netflix, and NVIDIA all had them.
- Sell only when the story breaks, never just because the price fell.
- Never use margin on titan-sleeve stocks.
- Size them like lottery tickets, buy in pieces, and let winners run past 3x.

## Next: your AI theories

Each theory runs through six questions:

1. **What changes?** State the effect of AI in one sentence.
2. **Who pays and who gets paid?** Every shift moves money from someone to someone.
3. **What's the second-order bottleneck?** What does this effect need that's in short supply?
4. **What's the timeline?** A 1–3 year effect and a 10-year effect call for different
   investments.
5. **Is it already priced in?** Check whether the obvious stocks have already run.
6. **How do we test it?** Name the number or event that would prove or disprove it.
