# Adobe (ADBE): AI implementation, not AI takeover

Written 2026-09-27. Held, bought at $240. Price $235.69 (latest trade, Fri 9/25 after-hours,
`quote.py`). Scorecard and valuation runs: `findings/2026-09-27-adobe-checkup.md`.
[V] filing/release · [C] calculation · [G] general/approximate · [O] opinion.

## The thesis in one sentence
AI does not replace Adobe; Adobe puts AI inside the tools and file formats a billion people
already use, and charges for it. That turns a seat business into a seat-plus-usage business,
while the market prices it as a company AI will shrink.

## The reframe: takeover vs. implementation
- **Takeover story (the market's):** AI image and video generators, Canva and Figma make Adobe's
  tools unnecessary; companies buy fewer seats; revenue shrinks.
- **Implementation story (this thesis):** once generation is cheap for everyone, the value moves
  to what AI can't copy: where the work happens (distribution), the file standards (PSD, PDF),
  professional control, and trust (commercially safe output). Adobe owns all four and is adding
  AI on top, so AI becomes something Adobe sells rather than something that replaces it.

This is your "distribution wins" principle applied to one company.

## The four legs, with evidence

### 1. Distribution AI can't copy
- 1 billion+ monthly active users, +20% [V]. Acrobat + Express 900M+ MAU, +25% [V].
- Free creative tier 100M+ MAU, +70% [V]: a funnel that pulls in new creators instead of losing
  them to Canva.
- PDF and PSD are industry standards. Enterprises run approval, legal and brand workflows on them [G].

### 2. AI inside the product, charged for
- AI-first ARR over $650M, +150% [V]. AI-influenced ARR over one-third of the business (FY25) [V].
- Firefly ARR (apps plus credit packs) +40% in one quarter; credit consumption accelerating,
  led by video [V].
- Acrobat AI Assistant users doubled quarter over quarter [V].
- **The key mechanism:** generative credits are usage-based. Revenue becomes seats × price
  **plus** credits used. If AI cuts seats, heavier usage by the seats that remain can offset
  it. This is the direct answer to the seat-compression bear case.

### 3. Second order: AI makes more content, and content needs a supply chain
- If AI multiplies how much marketing content companies make, the bottleneck moves to editing,
  brand consistency, approval, rights and delivery [O].
- Adobe sells that layer: GenStudio and Experience Platform ARR +20% [V]. The new CEO, Anil
  Chakravarthy, ran this business [V], which signals where Adobe is heading.

### 4. Implementing AI inside Adobe itself: weakest leg, not proven
- Revenue per employee ~$758K, +8% (FY25) [G]; headcount still rising ~2% [G].
- Operating expenses +15%, R&D +18%, faster than revenue's +13% [G]. So far Adobe is spending on
  AI, not saving with it.
- Test: operating expenses growing slower than revenue for two straight years. Until then, this
  leg is a hope [O].

## Keep vs. pass on (the airline test)
- Evidence Adobe keeps the gains: non-GAAP operating margin 44% in Q3, ~45% guided for FY26 [V];
  price increases and credit packs sticking; buybacks ~9% of shares a year at the current pace [C].
- Evidence against: GAAP margin 34.8% [V] and opex outrunning revenue. AI compute costs could eat
  the credit revenue [O].

## The six questions
1. **What changes?** Creative and document work shifts from hand-made to AI-assisted, and pricing
   shifts from per seat to per seat plus usage.
2. **Who pays, who gets paid?** Businesses pay more for content volume. Adobe gets paid if the
   work stays in its tools; pure generators get paid if it leaves.
3. **Bottleneck?** Not generation, which is getting cheap. The bottleneck is control, standards,
   brand safety and workflow, which is Adobe's ground.
4. **Timeline?** Usage revenue shows up within 1-3 years (already visible); the
   content-supply-chain leg plays out over 5-10.
5. **Priced in?** No. The price implies decline: ~10x free cash flow [C], and even zero growth
   gives ~7%/yr from here [C].
6. **How to test?** The confirm and break signals below.

## Bull and bear, from $235.69 (10-year model, `dcf.py`) [C]
- **Bull (11% growth, 40% FCF margin, 20x): ~29%/yr.** Credits and GenStudio become a second
  engine; buybacks shrink the share count.
- **Base (8%, 37%, 16x): ~23%/yr.** Fair value ≈ $465 at 12%, ≈ $579 at 9%.
- **Bear (4%, 34%, 13x): ~15%/yr.** AI helps, but pricing pressure caps it.
- **Takeover case (0%, 30%, 10x): ~7%/yr.** Growth stops.
- **Collapse case (-4%/yr, 25%, 8x): ~-1%/yr.** The takeover story is fully right.

Your earlier price zones stand: hold to ~$430-500, trim in pieces near ~$620, harder above ~$750.

## Confirm signals (additions to your list)
- Firefly and credit ARR keeps growing faster than total ARR.
- Paid conversion from the 100M free users (management gives numbers).
- Operating expenses grow slower than revenue (the internal-AI leg).
- GenStudio/Experience ARR stays above 15%.

## Break conditions (unchanged, plus two)
Existing: ARR under 6% for two quarters; Digital Media under 5%; management citing fewer seats or
lower renewals; operating margin under 30%; guidance cut; big stock-paid acquisition.
New:
- Credit/usage revenue stalls while seats fall. The offset isn't working.
- The new CEO (from 12/1/2026) pivots to large acquisitions or cuts buybacks at these prices.

## Ground truth to watch outside finance
- Designer and marketer forums (r/graphic_design, r/photoshop, r/marketing): are people switching
  to Canva/Figma/AI tools, or using Firefly inside Photoshop?
- Job postings: how often "Photoshop / Premiere / After Effects" still appears vs. "Figma / Canva"
  in design and marketing listings, tracked over time.
- Creators on YouTube and X with small followings describing their actual workflow.
