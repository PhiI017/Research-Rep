## AI & Market Cycles

The AI-power complex can experience major forced selling when leveraged funds are liquidating at the same time.

The important distinction is between selling caused by fundamental deterioration and selling caused by forced liquidation.

Bloom Energy is an example of a company that can become caught up in broader AI-power positioning and leverage even when the underlying fundamental thesis may not have changed.

Another AI fear cycle could happen within thirty to ninety days.

The important thing is to work out in advance which narratives will be used, who was influential last time, and how to measure whether a narrative is accelerating.

An accelerating narrative plus a levered market is the setup for a real correction.

The Chinese open-model threat was seen coming, but the speed at which the story spread was underestimated.

## Amazon

The important question around Amazon wasn't whether it would beat earnings, but how management would handle the capital-spending question on the earnings call.

Google had handled the issue badly, while Microsoft got ahead of it and stressed that spending could be redirected if demand didn't materialise.

The idea was that Jassy would follow the Microsoft approach.

This shows how important management communication around AI spending can become, even when the underlying earnings result itself is already expected.

## Finding Ground Truth

Uncover change in the world faster than others, connect it to companies that benefit, and look for ground truth where the world is confused.

AWS was found years ago by reading technology forums where developers talked about moving their companies to the cloud.

The useful information can often be found away from traditional finance.

Use earnings reports and media to work out what everyone already knows and what the single marginal driver is.

Then go to the people who actually work at these companies.

Developers on Reddit, X, and YouTube, often with almost no followers, can reveal whether AI is actually saving their employer money long before it reaches an earnings statement.

## The Attention Trade

"The attention trade" — where attention is, even when the information is false — could be among the biggest short-term trades of the next couple of years.

Individual accounts and small groups can potentially move markets short-term because everyone is connected and the thing driving the market is something most people don't understand.

Someone can move prices simply by projecting confidence, even if they're faking it.

This creates an environment where the spread of a narrative can become important in itself.

## AI & Distribution

Nobody can size AI yet.

The value of AI may only become clear once compute is abundant.

AI can be compared to electricity, where nobody could have forecast everything that would eventually be built once electricity became widely available.

The bigger question is what gets built once intelligence becomes abundant.

Distribution wins.

Once intelligence is free, designing something better gives you no advantage because everyone gets the best design simultaneously.

The winner is whoever already owns distribution, manufacturing, logistics, and moats that don't depend on intelligence.

Amazon is the major example because of twenty-five years of global logistics and infrastructure.

The goal is to work out who has the deepest distribution moats.

## AI Implementation

The signal to watch for is earnings language showing costs falling because of internal AI.

Booking.com reporting lower customer-service costs from its own AI tools is the type of signal to look for.

The important part is that this isn't just about twenty companies.

If AI starts producing measurable cost reductions across a large part of the index, that becomes a much bigger development.
